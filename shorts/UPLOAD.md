### Fact notes (safe phrasing used in the scripts)

**Monopoly**
- "Almost every top prize went to his network": the documented framing is that associates won almost all top prizes
  from 1995 to 2000. The script doesn't claim "no legitimate winners".
- "Over twenty million dollars": Jacobson's plea covered more than $20M in pieces. Media often say $24M; the DOJ said
  "more than $13M" at arrest.
- "An anonymous tip reached the FBI" (2000). Jacobson's sentence was 37 months. The children's-hospital piece (St.
  Jude, 1995) was attributed to Jacobson by prosecutors.

**New Coke**
- "About 200,000": sources say "nearly" or "no fewer than" 200,000.
- 1,500 calls a day (Coca-Cola, by June 1985).
- The "Chief Dodo" letter, and a psychiatrist hired to listen in (callers sounded like they'd lost a family member).
- "79 days": April 23 to July 10/11, 1985.
- Sip-test effect: a widely cited argument (Gladwell, *Blink*), phrased as "tends to win".
- Keough quote: "we're not that dumb, and we're not that smart." (Donald Keough, Coca-Cola president)
